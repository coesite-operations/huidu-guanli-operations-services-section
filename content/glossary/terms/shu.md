**Shu** (術, *technique* or *method*) is the practical means by which someone executes an authorized organizational task. Classical Legalist accounts associate Shu with administrative techniques for selecting, observing and evaluating officials; the management reading extends it to methods of meetings, negotiations, technical delivery and resource allocation.

## Instrumental rather than foundational

A technique should be evaluated against a specific task, applicable [Fa](fa.md), and the organization's [Dao](dao.md). If an authorized alternative better achieves the same accepted outcome at comparable cost and risk, the method can change without making the governing purpose or institutional rule disposable. Effective technique **does not become policy merely by succeeding**.

Huawei's concentration-of-force practice—placing substantial resources at a narrow consequential point—and the practice of valuing experience in difficult postings are examples discussed in this tradition. Their usefulness in another setting is a **case-specific hypothesis**; neither transfers automatically by imitating the name.

The classical distinction sometimes treats an administrator's diagnostic method as private while law is public. That is a context-specific claim, **not** permission to conceal a binding rule, evade oversight, violate access rights or deny a legitimate recipient evidence needed for receiving.

## Distinctions

[Fa](fa.md) states what binds; Shu is how the work gets done. [Shi](shi.md) is the position from which a method is applied; the same technique can succeed in one configuration and fail in another. [Dao](dao.md) supplies the direction that a method cannot establish for itself. Keep those roles distinct in Work and in explanatory prose.
