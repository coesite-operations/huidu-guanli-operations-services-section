**Dao** (道, *the Way*) names the governing direction of an organization: what it exists to accomplish and the standard by which its changing means are judged. In this glossary's management reading, it concerns **purpose**, not an individual leader's intuition or a mystical property.

## Decision role

Huawei's management language describes its direction as **customer-centricity**. The practical test of a stated direction is whether it changes a real decision: a profitable tactic that damages the organization's governing purpose may be rejected despite its apparent local success. A declaration on a poster, without an observable choice or accountable receiver, is not evidence that the direction governs.

The traditional formulation of a simple Way cautions against piling unrelated goals into one slogan. A short statement is useful only if the organization can apply it to conflicting choices. Simplicity of wording cannot replace a specific decision or beneficiary.

## Relations and boundaries

This corpus distinguishes four linked management readings:

- **[Dao](dao.md)**: the direction against which choices are judged
- **[Fa](fa.md)**: stable, published institutional rules that organize the work
- **[Shu](shu.md)**: practical techniques used within those constraints
- **[Shi](shi.md)**: advantage derived from an actual position or configuration

The terms describe different questions, not four certificates an organization automatically earns. Dao must inform the other three but cannot by itself prove that institutional rules are enforced, tactics are suitable, or a position is advantageous. [Wu wei](wu-wei.md) is a distinct practice of withholding unnecessary intervention when the operating configuration can carry the work.

Dao is not a dated strategy, tactical method, external mission-statement performance, or generalized claim that all outcomes are good. A purpose may persist while strategies change; an operational claim requires evidence of the actual decision and its consequences.
